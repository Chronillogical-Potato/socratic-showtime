THE QUESTION. Last week an AlphaGo co-author argued in MIT Technology Review that LLMs don't truly reason: AlphaGo found its famous Move 37 by pairing intuition with search, while an LLM's chain of thought is intuition run longer, with no checkable record, and often written after the fact. Others answered that humans have the same flaws, and that pencil, paper and the scientific method are what make human reasoning trustworthy. Explain the idea and let the viewer think it through. Take no side; end on the open question.

READ FIRST: ../shared/facts.md (every fact you may use, with sources), ../shared/alphago-lee-sedol-game2.sgf (the real game), ../shared/reasoning_traces.json (real reasoning text from a model run on this machine; it is being generated now, wait for it if it is missing). Facts outside facts.md need a source in your claims.md.

THE FOUR QUESTIONS (use exactly these, in this order; the video pauses at each one):
Q1 "Which part of AlphaGo found Move 37?"  A) The intuition  B) The search  C) Both.  Answer C: the policy network gave it a 1 in 10,000 chance; the search still chose it.
Q2 "Is a chain of thought a search?"  A) Yes  B) No  C) Partly.  Answer C: it can write "wait" and change course, and be sampled many times, but nothing checks each step.
Q3 "When Claude 3.7 Sonnet used a hint it was given, how often did its reasoning say so?"  A) About 90%  B) About 50%  C) About 25%.  Answer C (Chen et al., 2025).
Q4 "What would make a chain of thought trustworthy?"  A) Thinking longer  B) A record you can check, and checking it  C) A bigger model.  Answer B.
